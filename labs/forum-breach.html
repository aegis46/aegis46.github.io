Forum Breach / Linux incident response

Reconstruction of a Discuz forum compromise from unauthenticated database export and administrator impersonation through malicious plugin execution, webshell persistence, privilege escalation and database exfiltration.

CASE COMPLETEUACNGINXMYSQLSYSTEMD JOURNALLINUX FORENSICS
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

What happened

The attacker abused a PHP database-backup function without authenticating, obtained the administrator password hash and registered a specially crafted username to exploit a race condition. The resulting administrative session was used to reset the administrator password.

A PHP stager disguised as an image and a malicious Discuz plugin then wrote a persistent webshell into the application's data directory.

The webshell launched a Python PTY reverse shell to external infrastructure. Journal evidence placed privilege escalation at 2026-06-24 14:18:11 UTC.

Filesystem metadata placed the database dump at 14:28:52 and subsequent transfer-tool use at 14:29:34. A small text artefact was left in the root user's directory.

FORENSIC LIMITATIONS

What the evidence can prove

Nginx, MySQL and systemd journal records directly support the core exploitation, takeover and privilege-escalation timestamps. No shell-history or process-accounting record preserved the database-dump or transfer command lines.

The final two times therefore come from UAC bodyfile access times: 1782311332 for mariadb-dump/mysqldump, followed by 1782311374 for curl. These convert to the lab-accepted times of 14:28:52 and 14:29:34 UTC.

FINAL FINDINGS

Case conclusion

The evidence supports a continuous attack chain from unauthenticated database export through administrator impersonation, malicious plugin execution, webshell persistence, reverse-shell access, privilege escalation, database collection and exfiltration.

The investigation depended on correlating application logs with filesystem and live-response evidence. It also demonstrates why filesystem metadata must be described carefully: the database-dump and exfiltration times are strong, lab-confirmed access-time correlations rather than preserved command-execution records.
